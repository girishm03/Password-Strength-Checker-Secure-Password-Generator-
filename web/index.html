Shannon Information Entropy

Information entropy quantifies the unpredictability of a password. For a password of length \(L\) selected from character pool \(R\):

Entropy (bits) = L × log₂(R)
  • < 30 bits: Vulnerable instantly to commodity GPUs.
  • 30 - 50 bits: Weak; targeted brute force or dictionary attacks succeed quickly.
  • 50 - 70 bits: Moderate; safe from throttled web logins, weak against offline hashes.
  • 70 - 90 bits: Strong; resistant to high-end cracking rigs.
  • 90+ bits: Cryptographically sound; virtually uncrackable for centuries.
🛡️

NIST SP 800-63B Standards

The National Institute of Standards and Technology revolutionized credential security guidelines:

  • Length over complexity: 16+ characters or multi-word passphrases provide vastly superior protection compared to short strings with forced symbols.
  • Ban known breached passwords: Automatic checking against compromised databases is mandatory.
  • End periodic expiration: Forcing 90-day changes causes users to make predictable alterations (e.g. Spring2026! → Summer2026!).
  • No arbitrary composition rules: Character class mandates reduce natural password length without increasing actual entropy.
